## 15-minute walk-in audit checklist

Use this at the counter, in the bakkie, or on a short WhatsApp call. The aim is a chase list they can use today, even if they never buy.

### Before you go in (2 minutes)
- [ ] Confirm the suburb and the type of work (plumber, salon, panel shop, baker, and so on).
- [ ] Have the app open on your phone with sample invoices cleared or their name ready in Settings.
- [ ] Take a printed sample invoice and the pricing card (R750 + R249/mo, or R1,500 once-off).
- [ ] Do not ask for passwords, bank logins or their clients' private files. You only need names, amounts and due dates they are willing to share.

### Minute 0–3 — How money comes in today
- [ ] “When you finish a job, how does the customer get the invoice?” (WhatsApp photo, paper docket, email, Sage, nothing.)
- [ ] “How do you remind them if they have not paid?” (They remember, they feel awkward, the bookkeeper chases once a month, they let it slide.)
- [ ] “Do you take PayShap, EFT, or both? Are those details on every invoice?”

### Minute 3–10 — Who owes you this week
Write this down with them. Keep it to the last 30–60 days.

- [ ] Circle anything already overdue. Those are this week's WhatsApp reminders.
- [ ] Circle unpaid work that is due in the next seven days. Those get a polite payment request now, not after the due date.
- [ ] Note any invoice with no due date, no payment details, or only a voice note as “the invoice.”
- [ ] If they are VAT registered, confirm they want 15% VAT shown. If not, leave VAT off.

### Minute 10–13 — Two messages they can send today
- [ ] Pick one overdue and one unpaid invoice from the list.
- [ ] Offer the polite English or Afrikaans WhatsApp wording (the app generates both, plus a firmer version).
- [ ] They send it themselves. You do not message their clients.

### Minute 13–15 — The offer, only if the list was useful
- [ ] “If this would save you a few hours a month, I can set it up for you properly.”
- [ ] Quote both options. Setup includes loading their business, bank and PayShap details and showing them the weekly chasing habit.
- [ ] If they want time, leave your WhatsApp and offer to come back once they have sent this week's reminders.
- [ ] If they are not a fit (they already live in Xero and never chase, or they want a full bookkeeping service), thank them and, if they have a bookkeeper, ask for a referral instead.

### After the visit
- [ ] Log the date, suburb, owner name, number of overdue invoices, and whether they asked for setup.
- [ ] Do not store their client list any longer than needed. The product keeps data on their device.

## Objection notes (for you, not for a script)

- **“I already have Sage / Xero / Yoco.”** That's fine. This is for chasing, not for the full books. If they already chase well inside that tool, leave them be.
- **“I don't want another monthly fee.”** Point to the R1,500 once-off. The monthly option is for people who want you to stay close.
- **“My customers hate invoices.”** The message is a WhatsApp they already live in, with PayShap details included. They still choose when to press send.
- **“Will you message my customers?”** No. You never contact their clients.
